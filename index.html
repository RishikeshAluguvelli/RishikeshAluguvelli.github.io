Scaling Lab

Speculative decoding & tensor parallelism, from first principles

A decoding LLM's real bottleneck is a memory read. This lab studies the two orthogonal levers that beat it — amortize the read (speculative decoding) and shard it (Megatron-style tensor parallelism) — both built from scratch, both benchmarked, and both reported with the honest negative results.

1.86×EAGLE-3 speedup
0.84×n-gram — slower
−40.8%params / GPU at TP=2
  • Qwen2.5-7B on an H100 under vLLM: baseline 160.9 tok/s → EAGLE-3 299.8 tok/s (1.86×), while free-but-weak n-gram proposals lose 15% on open-ended prompts.
  • Megatron-style TP on 2×H200 cuts params-per-GPU 40.8% and peak memory 30.5% but runs at 0.62× speed — tensor parallelism's first job is memory, and speed is what it charges.
  • Includes the rejection-sampling proof that speculation leaves the output distribution exactly intact.

Attention Lab

MHA / GQA / MQA / MLA at decode, FlashAttention-2 & 3 at prefill

The attention layer is a memory problem wearing a compute costume. Two benchmarks isolate the two levers: cache less (architecture) at decode, and tile the score matrix (kernel schedule) at prefill.

13.1×MQA over MHA @32K
33×FA3 over naive @8K
40×less peak memory
  • H200, batch 16: at 32K context MQA reaches 13.1× MHA throughput, MLA 5.9×, GQA-8 3.5× — speedups grow with context exactly as the bandwidth model predicts.
  • At N=8192, FlashAttention-3 runs 2.03 ms vs naive 67.07 ms, and peak memory drops from 24.7 GiB to 626 MiB because the N×N score matrix is never materialized.
  • All three kernels agree to 5e-4 — FlashAttention is exact, not approximate.

KV Cache Lab

Cache latency, serving capacity, and low-bit quantization on a real 7B model

The KV cache is what makes LLM inference fast and what decides how many users one GPU can serve. Measured on Qwen2.5-7B / H100: the latency win, the memory bill verified against live tensors, and a full low-bit quantization study.

9.9×cached vs uncached @4K
585 → 1,560concurrent sequences
±1.03QJL bias slope at 1 bit
  • Cached decode is pinned at ~13 ms/token at any context while uncached doubles with every context doubling — 9.9× by 4K.
  • Rotating the cache before quantizing (QuIP/QuaRot-style incoherence processing) keeps 6-bit generation fluent where naive max-abs collapses into repetition loops — a 2.7× cache cut that nearly triples concurrent sequences on the same GPU.
  • Implements TurboQuant end to end: Lloyd–Max codebooks for N(0,1) plus a 1-bit QJL residual sketch that removes the shrinkage bias in attention logits (slope 0.63 → 1.03).

Autonomous Racing Stack

SLAM, particle-filter localisation, A* planning and local control on ROS

A complete ROS autonomy stack racing an Ackermann car with a 2D lidar around a 109 m track that is only ~1.4 m wide: online SLAM, AMCL localisation, A* global planning that re-routes around obstacles dropped on the racing line, and a 20 Hz sampling-based local planner — all validated end-to-end in a bundled, self-contained simulator.

0collisions over 1.4 laps
0.11 mmean localisation error
20 Hzlocal planning & control
  • Lidar-detected obstacles are fused into the global cost map with a time-to-live: the A* planner re-routes and the local planner threads the remaining gap — or holds safely in front when the track is fully blocked, resuming once the cells expire.
  • Robust by construction: tiered arc feasibility (full clearance → braking-horizon clearance → longest free arc), pose-based reverse-and-retry recovery, a follow-the-gap fallback, and a lap corridor that forbids routing the wrong way around the circuit.
  • The car has no wheel encoders — odometry is integrated from a kinematic bicycle model on the commanded steering/speed, with the SLAM scan matcher as an alternative source when speed tracking is unreliable.

MiniWorld 3D Navigation

PPO from pixels, plus a custom obstacle environment

A robot in a 3D room sees the world only through a first-person RGB camera — no coordinates, no map, no compass — and must find and reach a red goal box. Built up in stages: solve the stock task, design a custom obstacle environment, then randomize the goal so the agent has to genuinely search.

30/30fixed-goal success
83%random-goal success
0/30memoryless baseline
  • The first custom environment was unwinnable — the goal sat outside the room — yet its reward curve climbed 10×. A rising reward is not evidence the task is solvable; track success rate.
  • A memoryless policy cannot search: with a random goal, deterministic plain-CNN PPO loops forever on identical observations (0/30). Four stacked frames of memory took it to 83%.
  • Training success rate can hide a broken deterministic policy — evaluate the policy you intend to ship.

Multi-Agent Sheep Herding

Five dogs gather and drive twenty-five scattered sheep

Shepherding is control by repulsion: dogs cannot move sheep directly, only push them by proximity. One policy commands all five dogs from a single view of the field, so it has to learn spatial coordination — who covers which stray, how to form a driving arc — purely from reward.

77%curriculum PPO @ scatter 0.5
27%full scatter
0%without curriculum
  • Plain PPO scores 0% on the full scattered task regardless of reward shaping; a curriculum that widens the scatter as success passes ~40% gets there in ~9M steps.
  • Two-phase reward with a hysteresis gate — gather until the flock radius is tight, then drive from a Strömbom-style driving point behind the flock.
  • A scripted shepherd sets the 84% upper bound the learned policy is measured against.

MountainCar Continuous — A2C Study

Entropy bonus helps, short-horizon GAE breaks it

A controlled ablation on a classic sparse-reward exploration problem, where the engine is too weak to climb directly and greedy policies learn to do nothing. Two hyperparameters pull the algorithm in opposite directions.

~120ksteps to solve (best)
~90final reward, solved
~74gae_lambda=0.5, fails
  • An entropy bonus keeps the policy stochastic long enough to discover the momentum-building swing — fastest configuration by ~40k steps.
  • gae_lambda=0.5 biases advantage estimates toward short horizons, but the payoff for rocking backward arrives many steps later; the run degrades over training instead of improving.
  • gSDE (state-dependent exploration) is a strong default for continuous-control exploration.
